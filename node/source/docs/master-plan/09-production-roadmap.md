# Production roadmap and risks

## Proposed milestone sequence

This sequence adapts concept, design, implementation, validation and delivery work to an existing playable game. Q01 selected stabilization through Complex as the next milestone, with tier 7 design on paper alongside it. Q24 adopts the completion checklist and deferred scope below as the current baseline. Dates, estimates, owners and numerical validation thresholds remain unset.

Q21 sets older-save compatibility to best effort during development. For pending Mythril/global-transport changes, assess migration versus a documented fresh-start boundary rather than treating preservation of every older layout as mandatory. Retain working migrations unless a concrete change calls for otherwise; no saved games are reset by this plan.

**Agreed first delivery (Q01, November 2, 2026):** M1 through Complex, with M2 design work on paper in parallel where it does not depend on unmeasured balance. No tasks here have been estimated or assigned; the next design pass must turn the remaining specifications into sized tasks and measurable validation criteria.

Define M1's acceptance and deferred scope in Q24, then verify the selected hosted build when publication occurs; no release is made by this planning decision.

## Task format and priority

For each accepted task, record ID, player problem, desired outcome, scope, dependencies, acceptance check, evidence, owner, estimate and status. Until assigned, owner and estimate are **unassigned**. Use states: candidate, ready, in progress, blocked, verification and complete.

Prioritize progression blockers and save/data loss first, then risks threatening the selected milestone, then usability and balance, then polish. Q24 sets the milestone's completion gate and deferred list. Update [change history](../changelog/README.md) after completed meaningful work; preserve the distinction between a local result and publication.

When development candidates compete within the same priority, favor improvements to production optimization and geographic decisions (Q05). Assess construction, exploration and encounter work by how it supports those choices; this does not authorize new mechanics without their design review.
